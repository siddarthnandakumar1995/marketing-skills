## How Sid sounds

A B2B marketer who writes professionally but talks to you like a friend. Polished marketing vocabulary sits right next to casual asides and self-roasting. Upbeat, grateful, energetic, never cynical.

### Signature moves

1. **Self-roasting parenthetical asides.** Usually about sleep, timing, or irony.
   - "(SMH, NOT ME WRITING THIS POST @ 4:45AM ET😩)"
   - "(PS: Dang. I somehow ended up on the anti-branding side - the irony as a marketing individual, SMH...)"
2. **Thinking out loud to the reader.** Conversational pivots mid-post.
   - "Hmmm hold up, WDYM...", "OK but hear me out -", "OFC you guessed it right...", "So...what's my \"secret sauce\" so far? 🤔"
3. **CAPS for the moment that matters.** One or two words or a short phrase, not whole sentences.
   - "ACTIVELY LISTENING", "ICING ON THE CAKE MOMENT", "HELL YEA!", "DRUM ROLLS 🥁 🥁 🥁"
4. **Takeaways as a short dash list.** After a story or talk: 3-6 lines starting with "- ", each a short phrase or 1-2 sentences.
5. **A one-line punchline near the end**, often with a star or key emoji.
   - "Brand IS experience. ⭐", "Persistence is 🔑.", "\"WE\" are the brand ambassadors of a brand. ⭐"
6. **Gratitude and shout-outs.** Names the people who helped (mentors, speakers, teammates), with tags.
7. **A tension-then-payoff reveal**: builds up with "and..." or a drum roll, then the news.

### Sentence rhythm

- Short paragraphs: 1-3 lines, blank line between.
- Mix of full professional sentences and short punchy fragments ("Two teams. One board.").
- Opens with the moment or a personal hook, not a thesis statement.

### Vocabulary

**Uses naturally, so keep these:**
- Casual: stoked, gotta, kinda, dang, phew, SMH, EOD, WDYM, OFC, IMO, "oh boy", "one hell of a", "absolute ball", "fanboy moment", "steep learning curve", "secret sauce", "Player 1: Ready."
- Corporate B2B (part of the voice, don't strip it): enterprise, GTM, positioning, demand generation, brand narrative, storytelling, industry verticals, stakeholders, brand equity, high-impact, mission-critical, foundational, strategic engine, long-term growth.
- Shorthand: "@" for "at", "&" for "and", "v/s", "Drinx".

**Doesn't use:** "I'm humbled to announce", "thrilled", "delighted", "in today's fast-paced world". He says "stoked" and "super excited" instead.

### Emoji

- Used at the **end of a line**, rarely mid-sentence. Roughly 1 per paragraph, not on every line.
- Regulars: 🚀 (momentum, sign-offs) ⭐ (punchlines) 💡 (insight) 😩 (self-roast) 💯 (conviction) 🤔 (question) 🎉 (celebration) 👊 💪 (energy) 🔑 (key point).
- On opinion, stat, and tech posts, use fewer (2-4 total) so the point stays sharp.

### Formatting

- Dash lists ("- "), not numbered lists or bullets.
- Tags people by name where they're mentioned. In drafts, write `[@Name]` for the user to convert into a real tag.
- Hashtags: **exactly 5 keyword hashtags** at the very end (topic words like #ProductMarketing #B2BMarketing #BrandStrategy #EnterpriseAI #MBA). No filler tags.

### Sign-off rules (by post type)

- **Story posts**: end with a sign-off automatically. Rotate, don't repeat the same one every time:
  - "Onward & Upward! 🚀"
  - "Above & Beyond. 🚀"
  - "Here's to [the next chapter / more learning / ...]! 🎉"
  - "Three cheers to [...]! 🎉"
- **Opinion, stat, tech-in-marketing posts**: no sign-off unless asked. End on the punchline or a question for the comments.
- The user can always override: "add my sign-off" or "no sign-off".

## What performed best

From the sample of 9 posts:
- **Personal milestone posts** (new role, starting a program, end of a semester) did best: ~170-190 reactions and 24-47 comments.
- **Lecture and event recaps with takeaways**: ~100-115 reactions. The best ones added a personal twist (a debate he lost, a live demo, a fanboy moment).
- **Year-in-review reflection**: lowest (~70). General reflection without a concrete moment lands softer.

Takeaway for drafting: lead with a concrete moment, add a personal twist, and keep the takeaways specific.

## What would sound wrong for Sid

- Cynical, snarky, or dunking on people or companies
- Humble-brag openers ("I'm humbled and honored to announce...")
- Stiff, fully corporate posts with no aside, no humor, no personality
- Invented numbers or results
- Overlong walls of text with no line breaks
