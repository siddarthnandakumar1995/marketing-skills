# Post types

Four structures. Pick one, then adapt it. These are defaults, not cages: if the raw material fits better another way, follow the material.

---

## 1. Story post

**Use for:** milestones (new role, starting or finishing something), event, lecture, or conference recaps, lessons learned, team shout-outs.

**Structure:**
1. **Hook (1-2 lines)**: the moment, with personality. A concrete scene, a funny contrast, or the news itself.
   - "If my year had a magazine cover, it'd read: ..."
   - "Day 1 and BAM! We were put into groups for our summer project."
2. **Context (2-3 lines)**: where, who, why it mattered to you.
3. **What happened**: the story beat, with one personal twist (the irony, the surprise, the fanboy moment). This is where a parenthetical aside earns its place.
4. **Takeaways (optional, 3-6 dash lines)**: what you learned. Specific, not generic ("went in with a shortlist of 8 companies" beats "preparation matters").
5. **Punchline (1 line)**: the idea in one sentence.
6. **Gratitude (optional)**: tag the people who made it happen.
7. **Sign-off**: per the voice profile.
8. **Hashtags.**

**Pitfall:** general reflection with no concrete moment. Always anchor on a real scene.

## 2. Opinion post

**Use for:** a clear point of view on marketing, B2B, brand, careers, or AI.

**Structure:**
1. **Hook (1-2 lines)**: the stance, stated plainly. Something a reasonable peer might disagree with.
   - "Most B2B product launches are just press releases with a budget."
2. **Why I think this (2-4 lines)**: grounded in real experience ("Across 5 years in enterprise IT marketing, ...").
3. **The case (2-3 points)**: short paragraphs or a dash list. Each point is a reason, an example, or evidence.
4. **The fair counterpoint (1-2 lines)**: where the opposite view has a point. This builds credibility and invites comments.
5. **Punchline (1 line).**
6. **Question for the comments**: specific, easy to answer ("What's the one launch asset you'd cut first?"). Avoid "Thoughts?" on its own.
7. **Hashtags.** No sign-off unless asked.

**Pitfall:** a hot take with no experience behind it. If the user hasn't said why they believe it, ask.

---

## 3. Stat post

**Use for:** a post built around one real, sourced number.

**Structure:**
1. **Hook (1-2 lines)**: the number, in plain words, with its surprise.
   - "Only [X]% of B2B buyers say [Y]. [STAT + SOURCE NEEDED]"
2. **Source line**: who published it and when. Always. The user can put the link in the first comment.
3. **What it means (2-3 lines)**: the plain-language implication.
4. **My take (2-4 lines)**: why it matters for the reader's work, from the owner's experience.
5. **What to do about it (optional dash list, 2-4 lines).**
6. **Question for the comments.**
7. **Hashtags.** No sign-off unless asked.

**Rules:**
- One hero stat. Supporting numbers only if they're sourced too.
- Never invent, round up, or "estimate" a number. No source means `[STAT + SOURCE NEEDED]`.
- Pairs well with a visual: the stat as the image (see `platforms.md`).

## 4. Tech-in-marketing post

**Use for:** a tool, a workflow, or an AI shift and what it means for marketers.

**Structure:**
1. **Hook (1-2 lines)**: the before/after or the surprising result.
   - "I used to spend a full day building a content calendar. Last week it took 40 minutes."
2. **What it is, plainly (1-3 lines)**: explain it for a marketer, not an engineer.
3. **How I'm actually using it (3-5 lines or dash list)**: the real workflow, step by step. This is the credibility.
4. **What's real vs hype (2-3 lines)**: the limits, what still needs a human.
5. **Takeaway (1 line).**
6. **Question for the comments.**
7. **Hashtags.** No sign-off unless asked.

**Pitfall:** hype. No "game-changer", "revolutionary", "the future is here". Show the workflow and the result, and let the reader decide.

**Visual:** workflow posts benefit most from a simple diagram (see `platforms.md`).
